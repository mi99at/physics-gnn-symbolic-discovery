# Real-data roadmap

The current benchmark is synthetic by design. This roadmap gives a low-cost
path from the closed testbed to measurements that were not generated by the
physics graph.

## Stage 1 — a phone as a small laboratory

Use the free [phyphox](https://tools.phyphox.org/) app to collect accelerometer,
gyroscope, microphone, GPS, and pressure data. The app supports pendulum
frequency and Doppler-style experiments, remote browser control, and export to
CSV, TSV, or Excel.

Recommended first experiment:

1. Measure a pendulum at five or more lengths and three amplitudes.
2. Record at least ten trials per condition.
3. Keep raw timestamps and all sensor channels.
4. Record phone model, sensor sampling rate, length uncertainty, release
   method, temperature, and trial identifier.
5. Hold out complete lengths and a second phone for final evaluation.
6. Test whether the graph solver recovers period, frequency, length, and local
   gravitational acceleration with calibrated uncertainty.

This stage needs a phone, string, a small mass, a ruler, and a stable support.
It should be treated as a measurement study, not as evidence of a new law.

## Stage 2 — public orbital measurements

The [NASA/JPL Horizons system](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/manual.html)
provides accurate ephemerides through a browser and programmatic API without an
account. Export position, velocity, distance, period-related, and orbital
element fields for multiple bodies and time intervals.

Evaluation rules:

- split by celestial body or time interval, not by random rows;
- preserve units, reference frame, time standard, and observer definition;
- keep the Horizons query and raw response as immutable provenance;
- compare the GNN with direct numerical and symbolic orbital baselines;
- do not call an ephemeris-derived regularity a discovery without independent
  astronomical measurements and domain review.

## Stage 3 — public materials measurements

The [NIST Materials Data Repository](https://materialsdata.nist.gov/) can be
searched without an account and includes public community-contributed
materials data. Select records with raw measurements, units, calibration
details, and clear licensing. Candidate tasks include stress–strain,
temperature-dependent properties, and thermophysical relationships already
represented in the graph.

Split by specimen, material batch, or laboratory. Random row splits would leak
shared specimen conditions and overstate generalization.

## Protocol required for every real-data study

1. **Freeze the question.** Define target nodes, allowed observations, primary
   metrics, and exclusions before looking at final test results.
2. **Keep provenance.** Archive raw files, checksums, source URLs or instrument
   metadata, units, and every transformation.
3. **Model uncertainty.** Carry measurement uncertainty and missingness into
   training and report calibration, not only point accuracy.
4. **Use hard splits.** Hold out devices, specimens, bodies, time periods, or
   laboratories—not merely rows.
5. **Compare broadly.** Include target means, direct formulas, numerical
   solvers, flat neural baselines, and retrained graph ablations.
6. **Audit candidates.** Require symbolic derivation checks, prior shifts,
   negative controls, and sensitivity to nuisance variables.
7. **Replicate externally.** A candidate relation must survive new data from an
   independent instrument, source, or collaborator before any novelty claim.

## Resource order

Start with the phone experiment because it creates genuinely independent raw
measurements at almost no cost. Add JPL data next for scale and orbital
coverage. Use NIST materials data only after identifying a well-documented,
license-compatible subset. This order builds experimental discipline before
expanding the graph.
