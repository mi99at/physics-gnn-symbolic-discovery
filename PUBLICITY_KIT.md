# Physics GNN Symbolic Discovery — publicity kit

Use the stable project DOI in long-lived posts:

## One-sentence description

Physics GNN Symbolic Discovery represents classical mechanics as a graph of
quantities, equations, and laws, teaches a graph neural network to recover
hidden quantities through multiple routes, and audits every proposed
relationship before calling it a discovery.

## Plain-language summary

Most physics problems can be solved in more than one way. Mass, for example,
can be inferred from force and acceleration, momentum and velocity, kinetic
energy and velocity, weight and gravity, or density and volume. Md Minnatullah
encoded this idea as a graph: physical quantities are nodes, while equations
and laws connect them. A graph neural network learned to fill in missing
quantities in synthetic, internally consistent physical worlds.

The project also asks a harder question: when the network suggests a hidden
relationship, is it genuinely unexplained, already implied by known equations,
or only a correlation introduced by the data generator? A symbolic and
prior-shift audit forces the system to distinguish those cases. The current
paper does not claim new physics; it builds and tests the accountable reasoning
pipeline needed before that goal can be attempted on real measurements.

## Verified facts

- 305 graph nodes: 159 quantities, 131 equations, and 15 laws.
- 1,217 directed, feature-bearing edges.
- 650,000 synthetic physical worlds across 13 domains.
- Five exact input–target combinations held out from training.
- Archived held-out route scores of R² = 0.986–0.994.
- 400 candidate relationships screened and audited.
- 254 certified monomial consequences and 33 prior-sensitive artifacts.
- No candidate is presented as a newly discovered physical law.

## Author bio

Md Minnatullah is an independent researcher from Supaul, Bihar, India. Coming
from a low-income family without access to a laboratory or research funding, he
started with synthetic data and ordinary computing resources. His long-term
mission is to teach AI to reason across physics deeply enough to help humans
find genuinely new physical relationships, while requiring every candidate to
survive symbolic and empirical audit.

## Short social post

I see physics as a graph: quantities are nodes, equations are edges, and
solving means finding a path from what is known to what is hidden.

I built a 305-node physics graph and trained a GNN on 650,000 consistent worlds.
It solved five input–target routes that were held out during training, then a
separate audit checked whether mined relationships were known consequences or
data artifacts.

This is not a claim of new physics. It is a step toward an AI that must prove
what it finds.

## Responsible claim guide

Good wording:

- “The archived model learned five protected solution routes.”
- “Checkpoint interventions show sensitivity to node and edge information.”
- “The audit recovers known corollaries and identifies simulator-dependent
  artifacts.”
- “The long-term goal is AI-assisted discovery on real, independently collected
  data.”

Avoid:

- “The model discovered a new law of physics.”
- “The ablation study proves causality.”
- “The synthetic benchmark guarantees performance on experiments.”
- “The single archived GNN run establishes seed-independent performance.”
